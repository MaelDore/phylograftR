Format inputs prior to grafting

Maël Doré

2026-11-26


This vignette explains how to format the inputs needed to graft missing lineages: the missing_taxa_list that describes the taxa to graft and the constraints on where they can be grafted, and, optionally, the total_richness_per_clades that accounts for the species that are neither in the backbone nor in the list (i.e., unsampled species).

All the examples use the toy datasets shipped with the package: a backbone phylogeny of 30 tips (toy_backbone), tables in .csv format, and the corresponding R objects.

For a full analysis from scratch, see the main workflow tutorial: vignette("main_workflow").
For the tutorials listed by theme, see: vignette("phylograftR").


1/ What does grafting need?

The missing_taxa_list

Each missing taxon is assigned to a clade of the backbone phylogeny, defined by tips: a pair of tips whose most recent common ancestor (MRCA) is the clade, or a single tip for a terminal branch. There are two types of missing lineages, grafted with two functions:

Type Grafting function Clade item Meaning
Missing taxa graft_missing_taxa() Target_clade The taxon is grafted within its target clade.
Missing clades graft_missing_clades() Sister_clade The taxa sharing the same sister clade are grafted together, as one missing clade attached to the stem branch of their sister clade.

The missing_taxa_list is a list with the following items. Every item is named after the taxa ($Taxon_name):

Item Used for Content
Taxon_name Both Names of the taxa to graft. They must be unique and absent from the backbone.
Target_clade or Sister_clade Taxa / Clades One clade per taxon (a vector of tips).
Target_clade_name or Sister_clade_name Both (Optional) Names of the clades, to make outputs easier to read.
include_target_clade_stem Taxa (Optional) Can the taxon be grafted on the stem branch of its target clade?
Excluded_subclades Taxa (Optional) Subclades of the target clade where the taxon is known not to belong.
exclude_subclades_stem Taxa (Optional) Is the stem branch of each excluded subclade also excluded?
allowed_states Both (Optional) States of a stochastic map where the taxon can be grafted. See the tutorials on data types.

The backbone phylogeny: phy

The backbone phylogeny (phy) is the time-calibrated phylogeny on which the missing lineages are grafted: it must be rooted, binary (no polytomies) and ultrametric. All the clades used to define the constraints of the grafting are clades of the backbone phylogeny:

  • the target clade of a missing taxon: the clade within which it is grafted;
  • the excluded subclades of a missing taxon: clades nested within its target clade where it must not be grafted;
  • the sister clade of a missing clade: the clade of the backbone whose stem branch receives the missing clade.

Each of these clades can be a clade with several tips, or a single terminal branch, when it is defined by one tip only.

Clades are defined from the labels of the tips and the IDs of the nodes of the backbone:

  • a pair of tips defines the clade subtended by their most recent common ancestor (MRCA). Any pair of tips whose MRCA is the clade can be used, so a clade can be written in several ways;
  • a single tip defines the terminal branch of this tip;
  • a node ID defines the clade subtended by this internal node directly, with no need to pick tips. Node IDs are the integers that follow the tip IDs (1 to Ntip) and are displayed by ape::nodelabels().

The toy backbone is shown below with its tip labels in green and its node IDs in blue boxes. We will use both in the following Sections. For instance, the clade of node 34 is also defined by the pair of tips t8 and t7, whose MRCA is node 34, and the tip t5 alone is the terminal branch of t5.

# Plot the backbone with the tip labels in green, and the node IDs in label boxes
ape::plot.phylo(toy_backbone, cex = 0.7, no.margin = TRUE, tip.color = "green3")
ape::nodelabels(cex = 0.7, frame = "rect", bg = "lightblue", col = "darkblue")

The total_richness_per_clades

The backbone is incomplete, and the missing taxa of the missing_taxa_list are not necessarily all the missing species: some species may be unsampled, i.e., neither in the backbone nor in the list. Optionally, the grafting functions use a total_richness_per_clades to account for them: it gives the total number of species of some clades of the backbone (their richness), and their number of unsampled species is deduced from it:

total_richness = nb_backbone + nb_missing + nb_unsampled

with nb_backbone the tips of the backbone in the clade, nb_missing the taxa of the missing_taxa_list that belong to the clade, and nb_unsampled the unsampled species. This information is used to estimate the sampling fraction of the clades when fitting the birth-death models used to draw the divergence times of the grafted lineages (it requires method = "BD_clades" for the missing taxa). Unsampled species are only used to fit the rates: they are not grafted and do not need to be identified. Only their count is deduced from the total_richness values provided per clade:

  • For a clade with a total richness provided, nb_unsampled = total_richness - nb_backbone - nb_missing.
  • For a clade without a total richness (NA, or not listed), nb_unsampled is the sum of the unsampled species of the largest clades nested within it that have a total richness provided (or 0 if there is none). Unsampled species of nested clades are never counted twice.
  • Without total_richness_per_clades, all the clades are assumed to have no unsampled species.

The total richness is described in a table with the clades and their total richness, which is converted by dedicated functions: see Section 9.

The stochastic maps: simmap

Grafting can also be constrained by species attributes (e.g., traits or biogeographic ranges) described by stochastic character maps (simmap) of the backbone summarizing the evolutionary history of traits or ranges along branches. These stochastic maps are another input of the grafting functions, used with the allowed_states of the missing_taxa_list to further constrain grafting: each taxon can only be grafted on the pieces of the available branches that are in its allowed states. This combines with the other constraints:

  • For missing taxa, the available branches are those of the target clade, minus the excluded subclades, restricted to pieces in the allowed states.
  • For missing clades, the available branch is the stem branch of the sister clade, restricted to pieces in the allowed states.

Preparing these maps is thoroughly explained in a dedicated tutorial: vignette("prepare_stochastic_maps"). The use of allowed states is only briefly introduced in Section 6 of this vignette.

2/ Which way to build the list?

The missing_taxa_list can be obtained in three ways all returning the same kind of object, validated against the backbone. Whatever the way chosen, the list can then be edited by hand afterward for finer level of control.

You have… Use Section
A taxonomy of the backbone tips, and the taxonomic groups of the missing taxa build_missing_taxa_list_from_taxonomy() 3
A table that defines the clades directly (with pairs of tips or node IDs) build_missing_taxa_list() for a data.frame already in R, or import_missing_taxa_list() for an external file 4
A list written by hand in R, with node IDs defining the clades of interest convert_nodes_to_tips() 5
Any of the above: a list already built that you want to fine-tune further (stem branches, excluded subclades, taxa to keep…) Edit the list manually in R. This is available independently of the way the list was built (taxonomy, table/data.frame, or by hand) 8

The taxonomy is the most convenient way to start, as it only requires the names of the groups. Defining the clades from a data.frame or an external table offers more fine-tuning, as any node of the backbone can be used.


3/ Use a taxonomy: build_missing_taxa_list_from_taxonomy()

When the missing taxa are known by their taxonomic groups (families, genera, tribes…), they can be used to retrieve the defining tips of the clades of interest (i.e., target clades, excluded subclades, sister clades) directly from the taxonomy of the backbone tips.

When to use taxonomic tables?

Taxonomic tables are the go-to option for large phylogenies with neatly defined taxonomic levels (e.g., Family, Subfamily, Tribe, Genus). The taxonomy of the backbone is provided once, and each taxon to graft only needs its own taxonomic levels: the constraints (target clade, sister clade, excluded subclades) are written with the names of the taxonomic groups or of the taxonomic levels to use, and the clades are retrieved from the phylogeny. The same taxonomic group is therefore always the same clade, whatever the number of taxa, which avoids the typos and inconsistencies of clades written taxon by taxon, and the tables can be taken as such from a taxonomic database. The limit is that only the taxonomic groups of the backbone table can be used as clades: see Section 4 for finer control using any clade and Section 8 for editing the missing_taxa_list after building it.

Two tables are needed, and they can be imported from external files (text or Excel) with import_taxonomic_table():

  • the backbone table: one row per tip of the backbone, one column per taxonomic level (type = "backbone").
  • the missing taxa table: one row per taxon to graft, with its taxonomic groups, and the constraints written with taxonomic group names (type = "missing_taxa").

In the table of missing taxa:

  • Type states whether the taxon is grafted within a target clade ("taxa"), or as part of a missing clade ("clades").

Non-monophyletic groups. A taxonomic group is not always monophyletic in the backbone. The argument non_monophyletic controls what is done:

  • "exclude" (default) excludes the group from the grafting constraints of the taxa that use it;
  • "mrca" uses the clade of the MRCA of its tips (which includes tips of other groups);
  • "error" stops.

The groups, their MRCA node and their monophyly are reported in the attribute "taxonomic_groups", so you can check them.

The groups can only be those of the taxonomy. To use any clade of the backbone as a target, sister or excluded clade, define the clades directly with a data.frame (Section 4), or edit the list afterwards (Section 8).


4/ Build from a data.frame: build_missing_taxa_list()

Taxonomic groups are convenient, but they have limits: a clade that is not an identified taxonomic group cannot be used. build_missing_taxa_list() converts a data.frame into a missing_taxa_list, and gives you full control over the constraints: clades are defined directly on the backbone, from:

  • tips: a pair of tips whose MRCA is the clade ("t26, t18"), or a single tip;
  • node IDs: the ID of the node of the backbone, as shown by ape::nodelabels() (e.g., "34").

Any node of the backbone can therefore be a target, sister, or excluded clade, whatever the taxonomy. This option makes it much easier to fine-tune the grafting constraints, for instance to graft a taxon within a clade that is not named in any taxonomy, or to exclude a precise subclade. Tips and node IDs can be mixed between clades, but a single clade is defined by either tips or one node ID.

The data.frame has one row per taxon, and each column fills an item of the list:

  • Taxon_name: name of the taxon to graft.
  • Target_clade (or Sister_clade): tips used to retrieve the MRCA of the clade, separated by commas ("t26,t18"), or a node ID ("34").
  • Target_clade_name: (optional) name of the clade, used in printed reports ("Genus_1").
  • include_target_clade_stem: TRUE or FALSE. A blank cell means the default value of the grafting function.
  • Excluded_subclades: tips (or node IDs) of the subclades. Several subclades are separated by semicolons, and the tips of one subclade by commas ("t10,t19;t26,t4").
  • Excluded_subclades_names: names of the subclades, separated by semicolons ("Tribe_A;Genus_3").
  • exclude_subclades_stem: TRUE or FALSE with one value per excluded subclade, separated by semicolons ("TRUE;FALSE").
# ------ Step 1: Build the missing_taxa_list from a data.frame ------ #

# Write the table in R, with a mix of tips and node IDs (see the plot of the backbone)
my_table <- data.frame(Taxon_name = c("M1", "M2", "M3"),
                       Target_clade = c("34", "t5, t12", "t26, t18"),
                       Excluded_subclades = c("50", NA, "t10, t19; t26, t4"),
                       Excluded_subclades_names = c(NA, NA, "group_1; group_2"),
                       include_target_clade_stem = c(TRUE, FALSE, NA))

Any data.frame can be used, for instance a table loaded in R with read.csv(). The function removes extra spaces and treats blank cells and "NA" as missing values.

Import the table from an external file

The table can also be stored in an external file, which is convenient to edit it with a spreadsheet, to share it, or to keep it with the data of a project. import_missing_taxa_list() is equivalent to reading the file and giving the data.frame to build_missing_taxa_list(): it uses the same table format, the same checks and the same conversions of node IDs, and the clades are defined directly on the backbone, with the same flexibility. Text files (.csv, .tsv, .txt) and Excel files (.xlsx, .xls) are supported, and the format is chosen from the extension of the file.

missing_taxa_list <- import_missing_taxa_list(file = "my_missing_taxa.xlsx",
                                              phy = toy_backbone, sheet = "missing_taxa")

# Import any other format with a reader function (e.g., '.ods')
missing_taxa_list <- import_missing_taxa_list(file = "my_missing_taxa.ods", phy = toy_backbone,
                                              reader = readODS::read_ods)


5/ Convert node IDs of a list: convert_nodes_to_tips()

If you build the list directly in R, by hand (without a table), clades can be defined by node IDs too, but the grafting functions only accept tips. convert_nodes_to_tips() converts all the node IDs of the list into tips. It also checks the list against the backbone tree to make sure that it is valid: items named after $Taxon_name, node IDs and tips that exist in the backbone, no mix of tips and node IDs in a clade… It is used internally by build_missing_taxa_list(), import_missing_taxa_list() and build_missing_taxa_list_from_taxonomy(), so it is only needed for lists written by hand.

# A list with clades defined by node IDs, tips, or both
list_with_nodes <- list(
  Taxon_name = c("X1", "X2", "X3"),
  Target_clade = list(X1 = 34, X2 = c("t5", "t12"), X3 = "t26"),
  Excluded_subclades = list(X1 = list(group_1 = 50), X2 = NULL, X3 = NULL))

# Convert the node IDs into tips: the conversions are printed
list_with_tips <- convert_nodes_to_tips(list_with_nodes, phy = toy_backbone)
#> 2 node ID(s) converted into tips (a tip node gives its tip, an internal node gives one pair of tips with this MRCA node):
#>   - node 34 (internal node, 9 tips) -> t8, t7   [Target_clade: X1]
#>   - node 50 (internal node, 3 tips) -> t13, t27   [Excluded_subclades: X1]
list_with_tips$Target_clade$X1
#> [1] "t8" "t7"

An internal node is converted into a pair of tips whose MRCA is the node, chosen in a deterministic way. A clade that is already defined by tips in the list is always defined by the same tips, so the same clade has the same tips in all the objects. The total_richness_per_clades (Section 9) can be converted at the same time to keep this consistency: convert_nodes_to_tips(list, phy, total_richness_per_clades = richness).


6/ Species attributes: allowed states

Columns of the table can also restrict the states of a stochastic map where each taxon can be grafted. For instance, allowed_states gives one or several states for each taxon, separated by commas (blank cells mean no restriction for the taxon):

7/ Both types of missing lineages in one table

A single table can describe both types of missing lineages. With a column Type ("taxa" or "clades"), each row fills the clade item of its type (Target_clade or Sister_clade). Without Type, the clade item filled (Target_clade or Sister_clade) defines the type of the row. The builders (import_missing_taxa_list(), build_missing_taxa_list(), build_missing_taxa_list_from_taxonomy()) then return a named list with two elements, taxa and clades:

# ------ Step 1: A table with both types ------ #

both_table <- data.frame(Taxon_name = c("M1", "M2", "M3", "M4"),
                         Type = c("taxa", "taxa", "clades", "clades"),
                         Target_clade = c("t26, t18", "t5, t12", NA, NA),
                         Sister_clade = c(NA, NA, "t10, t19", "t10, t19"),
                         Excluded_subclades = c("t26, t4", NA, NA, NA))

# ------ Step 2: Build the lists ------ #

lists <- build_missing_taxa_list(data = both_table, phy = toy_backbone)
names(lists)
#> [1] "taxa"   "clades"
lists$taxa$Taxon_name      # Taxa to graft with graft_missing_taxa()
#> [1] "M1" "M2"
lists$clades$Taxon_name    # Taxa to graft with graft_missing_clades()
#> [1] "M3" "M4"
lists$clades$Sister_clade  # M3 and M4 have the same sister clade: they form one missing clade
#> $M3
#> [1] "t10" "t19"
#> 
#> $M4
#> [1] "t10" "t19"

This list(taxa, clades) is the format expected by graft_missing_taxa_and_clades(), which grafts both types in one run, see vignette("combine_missing_taxa_and_clades"). The functions that check the grafting before or after (check_grafting_plan(), check_grafting_constraints(), extract_grafting_info(), extract_sampling_info()) and convert_nodes_to_tips() accept it too. They return a named list with the result of each type, see vignette("check_grafting"). To graft the types separately, use each element with the matching function: lists$taxa for graft_missing_taxa() and lists$clades for graft_missing_clades().


8/ Edit the list manually

The missing_taxa_list is a regular R list. Whatever the way it was built (taxonomy, data.frame, external table, or by hand), it can be edited directly in R before grafting, and the same edit can also be applied to a list of both types of taxa (Section 7), element by element. This is the final level of fine-tuning and control: any item can be changed for any taxon.

The rules to keep in mind are:

  • every item must remain named after $Taxon_name and have one element per taxon (in the same order);
  • clades must be defined by tips: if you use node IDs, convert them with convert_nodes_to_tips() (see Section 5);
  • the list of a given type must keep its clade item (Target_clade for the missing taxa, Sister_clade for the missing clades).
# ------ Step 1: Edit the list ------ #

# Start from the list of taxa built from the taxonomy (Section 3)
taxa_list <- missing_taxa_lists$taxa

# Allow New_sp3 to be grafted on the stem branch of its target clade
taxa_list$include_target_clade_stem[["New_sp3"]] <- TRUE

# Add an excluded subclade (and exclude its stem) for New_sp4
taxa_list$Excluded_subclades[["New_sp4"]] <- list(Gen_1 = c("t13", "t27"))
taxa_list$exclude_subclades_stem[["New_sp4"]] <- c(Gen_1 = TRUE)

# Remove a taxon: subset all the items with the same selection
keep <- taxa_list$Taxon_name != "New_sp7"
taxa_list <- lapply(taxa_list, function (item) item[keep])

# ------ Step 2: Check the edited list ------ #

# The structure of the list and the availability of branches for grafting
# can be checked with check_grafting_plan() (see `vignette("check_grafting")`)
plan <- check_grafting_plan(phy = toy_backbone, missing_taxa_list = taxa_list, verbose = FALSE)
plan[, c("Taxon_name", "n_branches", "feasible")]
#>   Taxon_name n_branches feasible
#> 1    New_sp1          8     TRUE
#> 2    New_sp2         22     TRUE
#> 3    New_sp3         33     TRUE
#> 4    New_sp4         20     TRUE
#> 5    New_sp5         12     TRUE
#> 6    New_sp6          1     TRUE

The checks stop with an explicit message if the edited list is not valid, for instance if a clade is defined by a node ID, or if an item does not have one element per taxon.


9/ The total richness of clades

The backbone is incomplete, and the missing taxa of the list are not necessarily all the missing species: other species may also be unsampled (neither in the backbone nor in the missing_taxa_list). Optionally, the grafting functions use a total_richness_per_clades to account for them: it gives the total number of species of some clades of the backbone (their richness), and their number of unsampled species is deduced from it:

total_richness = nb_backbone + nb_missing + nb_unsampled

with nb_backbone the tips of the backbone in the clade, nb_missing the taxa of the missing_taxa_list that belong to the clade, and nb_unsampled the unsampled species. This information is used to estimate the sampling fraction of the clades when fitting the birth-death models used to draw the divergence times of the grafted lineages (it requires method = "BD_clades" for the missing taxa). Unsampled species are only used to fit the rates: they are not grafted and do not need to be identified. Only their count is deduced from the total_richness values provided per clade:

  • For a clade with a total richness provided, nb_unsampled = total_richness - nb_backbone - nb_missing.
  • For a clade without a total richness (NA, or not listed), nb_unsampled is the sum of the unsampled species of the largest clades nested within it that have a total richness provided (or 0 if there is none). Unsampled species of nested clades are never counted twice.
  • Without total_richness_per_clades, all the clades are assumed to have no unsampled species.

A table with the clades and their total richness is converted by build_total_richness_per_clades() (internal data.frame) or import_total_richness_per_clades() (external file). Both need the missing_taxa_list (or the list(taxa, clades)) to check the richness values against the species of the backbone and of the list, and to make sure that the clades are defined by the same tips.

# ------ Step 1: A table with the clades and their total richness ------ #

# Clades are written with their taxonomic names, as in the list built from the taxonomy.
# Use NA if the total richness is unknown
richness_table <- data.frame(Clade = c("Family_A", "Family_B", "Subfam_B1", "Tribe_B2"),
                             total_richness = c(25, NA, 14, 8))

# ------ Step 2: Build the total_richness_per_clades ------ #

# Check against the backbone and the lists of both types
richness_per_clades <- build_total_richness_per_clades(data = richness_table, phy = toy_backbone,
                                                       missing_taxa_list = missing_taxa_lists,
                                                       verbose = FALSE)

# With both types, the output has one element per type. Clades are defined by the same tips as in the lists
names(richness_per_clades)
#> [1] "taxa"   "clades"
str(richness_per_clades, max.level = 2)

The attribute "richness_table" gives the number of species in the backbone, the missing species of the list, and the unsampled species deduced from the total richness, and the resulting sampling fraction of each clade. It can be removed with return_richness_table = FALSE.

These values are expectations, computed before any grafting. The table assumes that all the taxa to graft end up in the clade where they belong. However, where taxa are actually grafted varies among the grafted phylogenies (and among the stochastic maps, if any): a taxon can be grafted on the stem of its target clade, which is outside of the clade, or within a clade nested in its target clade, which changes the richness of these nested clades. The effective sampling fractions of the grafted phylogenies may therefore differ from the expected ones. They can be extracted a posteriori with extract_sampling_info(), using the same total_richness_per_clades, on the output of the grafting functions (see the tutorial on checks: vignette("check_grafting")).

Define the clades of the richness table by node IDs

The clades of the total richness table can be defined by tips or node IDs too (see Section 4). When the objects are written by hand in R, convert_nodes_to_tips() converts the node IDs of the missing_taxa_list and of its total_richness_per_clades in the same call (see Section 5), so that the same clade is defined by the same tips in both objects:

# A missing_taxa_list written by hand with node IDs and tips (see Section 5)
list_with_nodes <- list(
  Taxon_name = c("X1", "X2"),
  Target_clade = list(X1 = 34, X2 = c("t5", "t12")))

# A total_richness_per_clades written by hand: node 34 is the clade of X1
richness_with_nodes <- list(Target_clade = list(34, c("t5", "t12")),
                            total_richness = c(14, 8))

# Convert both objects at the same time
converted <- convert_nodes_to_tips(missing_taxa_list = list_with_nodes, phy = toy_backbone,
                                   total_richness_per_clades = richness_with_nodes)
#> 1 node ID(s) converted into tips (a tip node gives its tip, an internal node gives one pair of tips with this MRCA node):
#>   - node 34 (internal node, 9 tips) -> t8, t7   [Target_clade: X1 | total_richness_per_clades$Target_clade: clade 1]
names(converted)
#> [1] "missing_taxa_list"         "total_richness_per_clades"

# Node 34 is defined by the same tips in the list and in the total richness
converted$missing_taxa_list$Target_clade$X1
#> [1] "t8" "t7"
converted$total_richness_per_clades$Target_clade[[1]]
#> [1] "t8" "t7"

With both types of missing lineages, convert_nodes_to_tips() takes the list(taxa, clades) and a total_richness_per_clades of the same form, and converts both parts with a shared record of the clades.

The richness is provided to the grafting functions in the argument total_richness_per_clades: the list of the matching type to graft_missing_taxa() or graft_missing_clades(), and the list(taxa, clades) to graft_missing_taxa_and_clades().


10/ Common input errors

The functions check the inputs against the backbone and stop with explicit messages, before any grafting. Below are the most common cases.

# Helper to display the message of an error
show_error <- function (expr) cat("Error:", conditionMessage(tryCatch(expr, error = function (e) e)), "\n")

# A tip that is not in the backbone (e.g., a typo, or a wrong separator)
show_error(build_missing_taxa_list(data.frame(Taxon_name = c("M1", "M2"), Target_clade = c("t1, t999", "t5")),
                                   phy = toy_backbone, verbose = FALSE))
#> Error: Some tips of 'Target_clade' are missing from the backbone phylogeny (check the spelling of the tips, or the separators):
#>   - M1 (row 1): t999

# Tips and a node ID mixed in the same clade
show_error(build_missing_taxa_list(data.frame(Taxon_name = c("M1", "M2"), Target_clade = c("t1, 34", "t5")),
                                   phy = toy_backbone, verbose = FALSE))
#> Error: 'Target_clade' of 'M1' mixes node IDs and tips in the same clade: t1, 34.
#> A clade is defined either by tips (labels with at least one other character than digits), or by one node ID, not both.

# A node ID that does not exist
show_error(build_missing_taxa_list(data.frame(Taxon_name = c("M1", "M2"), Target_clade = c("t8, t7", "900")),
                                   phy = toy_backbone, verbose = FALSE))
#> Error: 'Target_clade' of 'M2' has an invalid node ID: 900. Node IDs of 'phy' are integers between 1 and 59 (1 to 30 for the tips).

# Duplicated names of taxa
show_error(build_missing_taxa_list(data.frame(Taxon_name = c("M1", "M1"), Target_clade = c("t8, t7", "t5")),
                                   phy = toy_backbone, verbose = FALSE))
#> Error: 'Taxon_name' must be unique. Duplicated: M1

# A taxon to graft that is already a tip of the backbone
show_error(build_missing_taxa_list(data.frame(Taxon_name = c("t5", "M2"), Target_clade = c("t8, t7", "t5")),
                                   phy = toy_backbone, verbose = FALSE))
#> Error: 'Taxon_name' must be absent from the backbone phylogeny (these are taxa to graft). Present in 'phy': t5

# A taxon without clade
show_error(build_missing_taxa_list(data.frame(Taxon_name = c("M1", "M2"), Target_clade = c("t8, t7", NA)),
                                   phy = toy_backbone, verbose = FALSE))
#> Error: 'Target_clade' is empty for: 'M2' (row 2)

Node IDs are recognized as text made only of digits: tip labels of the backbone must therefore include at least one other character than digits. If some tips are only digits, rename them first.


Next steps

With a missing_taxa_list (and optionally a total_richness_per_clades), you are ready to graft:

  • the missing taxa: graft_missing_taxa(), see vignette("graft_missing_taxa");
  • the missing clades: graft_missing_clades(), see vignette("graft_missing_clades");
  • both types in one run: graft_missing_taxa_and_clades(), see vignette("combine_missing_taxa_and_clades").

The grafting plan can be checked beforehand with check_grafting_plan(): vignette("check_grafting").

If you wish to use species attributes to further constraint grafting based on the evolutionary history of traits and/or biogeographic ranges, you will need to provide stochastic character maps under the simmap argument to grafting functions. Please refer to the dedicated tutorials explaining how to:

  • prepare the stochastic maps: see vignette("prepare_stochastic_maps");
  • use biogeographic ranges as constraints (i.e., GIG = geographically-informed grafting): see vignette("biogeographic_data");
  • use several traits as constraints: see vignette("multiple_traits").
